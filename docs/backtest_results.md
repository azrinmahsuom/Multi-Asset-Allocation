# Backtest results

Generated by `uv run python -m analysis.pipeline backtest`; do not edit by hand. Rules are in `src/analysis/backtest/engine.py` and `robustness.py` and were fixed before results were computed. Risk-free rate for Sharpe and Sortino: the SGD one-month rate (SORA Index from August 2005, compounded SGD interbank overnight before; MAS). All returns in SGD, net of fund TER, dividend withholding tax and trading costs at the USD 25,000 minimum; rebalancing every December and when a weight drifts more than 10 points.

**Adopted allocation:** 25.4% global equities (unhedged) and 74.6% SGS: risk-parity weights, adopted after the holdout evaluation in `docs/strategy_design.md`, where the three profiles selected the same mix and were merged. The in-sample picks that preceded it are shown for reference.

**In-sample caveat:** the allocation and its limits were chosen using SGD history that overlaps this backtest, so these results are not out of sample. The holdout evaluation is the out-of-sample check.

Withholding-tax drag on equities in the index layer averaged 0.27% a year (trailing dividend yield x 15%). The ETF layer's fund prices already include TER and fund-level withholding tax.

## What drove returns

Annualised sleeve returns over the index layer: equities 6.8%, SGS 2.6%, SGD cash 1.1% (nominal SGD). For the adopted allocation, equities contributed about 1.7% a year and SGS 1.9%. The 10-year SGS yield fell from 5.30% at the start to 0.81% (2020-10) and ended at 2.35%: much of the bond return came from falling yields, which cannot repeat from today's starting level. Forward bond returns are better anchored to the current yield.

## Caveats

- About 28 years of SGD history; the ETF layer starts in November 2011 and has no 2008 crisis.
- Index-layer equities are developed markets only (no emerging markets before the ETF layer); the withholding-tax drag uses the 15% treaty rate on the whole dividend yield, an approximation; TERs are secondary-source figures (to_verify).
- Trading costs use US-listed ETF spreads and a US fee schedule as stand-ins for LSE and SGX trading; costs are small at annual rebalancing, and stress tests at 2x and 3x spreads change little. Stand-ins are to be replaced before README numbers are final.
- Benchmark global bonds use US Treasuries hedged to SGD before July 2019, with no fund fee.
